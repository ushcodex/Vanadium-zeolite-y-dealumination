# ABSTRACT

Vanadium contamination reduced Zeolite Y activity in residue fluid catalytic cracking (RFCC). Proposed mechanisms attributed framework damage to vanadic acid or steam hydrolysis. This study compared vanadic acid (H₃VO₄) and water at a model Zeolite Y Brønsted acid site using a 22-atom AlSi₄O₄H₁₃ cluster. Initial geometries were screened with GFN2-xTB, optimised with B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP, and checked with PBE0 single-point calculations. Harmonic Gibbs energies were calculated at 298.15 and 1003.15 K. B3LYP and PBE0 favoured vanadic acid electronically by 14.69 and 14.44 kJ mol⁻¹, respectively, while harmonic Gibbs energies favoured water by 11.82 and 66.36 kJ mol⁻¹ at the two temperatures. Vanadic acid formed Al···O and V···O contacts of 1.947 and 2.274 Å; water formed two hydrogen bonds with H···O distances of 1.587 and 1.825 Å. A formal competitive-site estimate using regenerator partial pressures gave a water-to-vanadic-acid site-fraction ratio of 5.7 × 10⁷ to 5.7 × 10⁸. The GFN2-xTB search yielded a 160.70 kJ mol⁻¹ candidate barrier for the first Al–O cleavage step; Appendix A records the DFT frequency and water-path displacement assessments. Electronic binding and high-temperature site occupation favoured different adsorbates. Larger pore-aware models and validated DFT reaction paths were recommended.
