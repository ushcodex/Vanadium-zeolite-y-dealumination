# 3.0 METHODOLOGY

## 3.1 Computational Workflow and Resources

The calculations followed the four objectives in order. The local Zeolite Y model and initial adsorption geometries were prepared first. Accepted structures were then refined, and electronic adsorption energies were calculated. Candidate transition states were searched for before the harmonic thermochemistry and conditional site-occupation estimate were evaluated. Figure 3.1 summarises this workflow.

[FIG: fig3_1_workflow.png | Figure 3.1: Two-stage computational workflow used in this study | Source: author]

GFN2-xTB calculations were run with ORCA 6.1.0 on a Dell Latitude E6430 laptop with 16 GB of memory and one processor core. The DFT calculations were run with ORCA 6.1.1 on a cloud virtual machine using four MPI processes (Neese, 2025). Molecular structures were prepared and checked with Spartan and Avogadro. Python scripts parsed the ORCA output files and supported the calculation tables.

## 3.2 Zeolite Y Cluster and Adsorbate Models

The model was a gas-phase cluster with formula AlSi₄O₄H₁₃, containing 22 atoms. It represented one framework aluminium atom, four neighbouring silicon atoms, four bridging oxygen atoms, twelve terminal hydrogen atoms, and one proton on a bridging oxygen. The protonated oxygen formed the model Brønsted acid site, while the terminal hydrogen atoms capped bonds at the cluster boundary. The cluster, vanadic acid, and water were treated as neutral singlets.

The twelve terminal hydrogen atoms were held fixed during geometry optimisation. The aluminium atom, four silicon atoms, bridging oxygen atoms, and Brønsted proton were allowed to relax. Vanadic acid was modelled with one V=O bond and three V–OH groups, while water was modelled as H₂O. Several starting orientations of each adsorbate were prepared around the acid site before screening. Figure 3.2 shows the cluster and isolated reference species.

## 3.3 Structure Refinement and Adsorption Energies

The isolated cluster, vanadic acid, and water were first optimised with GFN2-xTB. Adsorption complexes were generated by placing each adsorbate in several orientations near the Brønsted proton and optimising the resulting structures. The lowest-energy converged structures were carried forward for DFT refinement. This screening step supplied starting geometries and was not used as the final DFT adsorption-energy estimate.

The accepted reference structures and adsorption complexes were re-optimised with B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP. The calculations used the RIJCOSX approximation, the def2/J auxiliary basis, the DefGrid3 integration grid, tight self-consistent-field convergence, and tight geometry convergence. Harmonic frequencies were calculated for the optimised structures at the same level. PBE0-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP single-point calculations were performed on the B3LYP-optimised geometries of the isolated reference species and the two adsorption complexes; the PBE0 geometries were not re-optimised.

The electronic adsorption energy was calculated as:

ΔEₐdₛ = E(complex) − [E(cluster) + E(adsorbate)]
(3.1)

A negative value indicated that the complex had a lower electronic energy than the separated cluster and adsorbate. The relative adsorption-energy difference was calculated as:

ΔΔE = ΔEₐdₛ(H₃VO₄) − ΔEₐdₛ(H₂O)
(3.2)

A negative ΔΔE indicated a more negative electronic adsorption energy for vanadic acid. Counterpoise calculations were attempted to estimate basis-set-superposition error, but they did not produce a complete set of converged fragment energies. No counterpoise-corrected values were therefore used.

## 3.4 Transition-State Searches

Candidate reaction paths were explored for the vanadic-acid route and for a water-hydrolysis control. GFN2-xTB nudged elastic band calculations were followed by eigenvector-following transition-state optimisations (Henkelman & Jónsson, 2000). Frequency calculations and displacements in both directions along each candidate imaginary mode were used to test whether the structure connected the expected neighbouring minima.

A GFN2-xTB transition-state candidate was accepted only when the optimisation converged, the frequency calculation gave one chemically meaningful imaginary mode, and the two displaced structures relaxed towards different minima. The accepted Tier 1 candidate for the first framework Al–O cleavage was retained for DFT assessment. The corresponding DFT optimisation was checked by its convergence and vibrational frequencies. Unconverged structures and candidates that failed the mode or displacement checks were not treated as validated transition states.

## 3.5 Gibbs Energies and Conditional Site Occupation

Harmonic Gibbs energies were obtained from ORCA frequency calculations at 298.15 K and 1003.15 K. The higher temperature represented an RFCC regenerator condition of about 730 °C. The Gibbs energy of adsorption was calculated from the complex, cluster, and isolated adsorbate:

ΔG°ₐdₛ = G(complex) − G(cluster) − G(adsorbate)
(3.3)

The values used ideal-gas, rigid-rotor, harmonic-oscillator thermochemistry at a standard pressure of 1 atm. The free-energy difference was evaluated separately from the electronic adsorption-energy difference because thermal corrections affected the relative ordering.

A formal competitive-site estimate was made at 1003.15 K using the regenerator partial pressures discussed by Wormsbecher et al. (1986). Twenty per cent steam at 2 atm total pressure corresponded to a water partial pressure of 0.4 atm. The reported range of 1–10 ppm vanadic acid corresponded to partial pressures of 2 × 10⁻⁶ to 2 × 10⁻⁵ atm. For each species, the dimensionless standard equilibrium constant and the competitive site fraction were calculated as:

K°ᵢ = exp[−ΔG°ᵢ/(RT)]
(3.4)

θᵢ = K°ᵢ(pᵢ/p°) / [1 + Σⱼ K°ⱼ(pⱼ/p°)]
(3.5)

This calculation assumed one equivalent site, ideal-gas standard states, and no interaction between adsorbed molecules. It was used as a conditional thermochemical scenario, not as a definitive prediction of occupancy in the zeolite pore.

## 3.6 Data Handling and Acceptance Criteria

The ORCA input files, output files, final geometries, and calculation registers were retained in the repository. Reported adsorption energies and thermochemical values were checked against the output logs and the calculation register. The same reference cluster and isolated adsorbate energies were used when calculating each adsorption energy.

A minimum was accepted when the geometry optimisation converged and the frequency calculation showed no chemically meaningful imaginary mode. Frequencies below 20 cm⁻¹ were treated as numerical noise only when the associated displacement did not describe a reaction; borderline modes were assessed using their displacement behaviour. The cluster-reference frequency of −4.64 cm⁻¹ met the low-frequency-noise criterion. A transition-state candidate required one chemically meaningful imaginary frequency and a successful two-sided displacement check. These criteria separated the GFN2-xTB screening result from the DFT pathway assessment.
