# 3.0 METHODOLOGY

## 3.1 Computational Workflow and Resources

The calculations modelled vanadic acid and water at one Zeolite Y Brønsted acid site. They evaluated optimised structures, electronic adsorption energies, candidate reaction paths, and harmonic thermochemistry. GFN2-xTB provided the Tier 1 screening calculations, which were run with ORCA 6.1.0 on a Dell Latitude E6430 laptop with 16 GB of memory and one processor core. Tier 2 DFT calculations were run with ORCA 6.1.1 on a cloud virtual machine using four MPI processes (Neese, 2025). Molecular structures were prepared and checked with Spartan and Avogadro; Python scripts processed ORCA outputs and calculation registers. ## 3.2 Zeolite Y Cluster and Adsorbate Models

The gas-phase cluster had formula AlSi₄O₄H₁₃ and contained 22 atoms. It represented one framework aluminium atom, four neighbouring silicon atoms, four bridging oxygen atoms, twelve terminal hydrogen atoms, and one proton on a bridging oxygen. The protonated oxygen formed the model Brønsted acid site, and terminal hydrogens capped bonds at the cluster boundary. The cluster, vanadic acid, and water were treated as neutral singlets.

The twelve terminal hydrogen atoms were held fixed during geometry optimisation. Aluminium, silicon, bridging oxygen atoms, and the Brønsted proton were allowed to relax. Vanadic acid was modelled with one V=O bond and three V–OH groups; water was modelled as H₂O. Several starting orientations of each adsorbate were prepared near the acid site. ## 3.3 Electronic Structure and Adsorption Energies

The isolated cluster, vanadic acid, and water were optimised with GFN2-xTB. Adsorption complexes were generated by placing each adsorbate in several orientations near the Brønsted proton and optimising each structure. Selected structures and reference species were optimised with B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP using the RIJCOSX approximation, def2/J auxiliary basis, DefGrid3 integration grid, tight self-consistent-field convergence, and tight geometry convergence. Harmonic frequencies were calculated at the same level. PBE0-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP single-point energies were evaluated on the B3LYP-optimised geometries of the isolated reference species, pre-reaction complexes, and converged post-adsorption minima.

The electronic adsorption energy was calculated using Eq. (3.1):

ΔE_{ads} = E(complex) − [E(cluster) + E(adsorbate)]
(3.1)

Equation (3.1) defines the separated cluster and optimised adsorbate as the reference state. A negative ΔE_{ads} indicates that the complex has lower electronic energy than the separated species. The relative adsorption-energy difference was calculated using Eq. (3.2):

ΔΔE_{ads} = ΔE_{ads}(H₃VO₄) − ΔE_{ads}(H₂O)
(3.2)

Equation (3.2) assigns negative values to an electronic preference for vanadic acid. Appendix B.2 shows the numerical substitutions for both DFT levels. The adsorption energies reported in Chapter Four are direct electronic values; Appendix A.2 records the counterpoise fragment calculations.

## 3.4 Transition-State Searches

Candidate reaction paths were explored for the vanadic-acid route and a water-hydrolysis control. GFN2-xTB nudged elastic band calculations were followed by eigenvector-following transition-state optimisations (Henkelman & Jónsson, 2000). For each candidate, frequency analysis located the imaginary mode; structures displaced in both directions along that mode were reoptimised to test whether the candidate connected different minima. The pre-reaction complexes are denoted V-PRC (vanadic acid) and W-PRC (water). V-I1 and V-I2 denote successive vanadic-acid pathway states, W-P denotes the water-path post-adsorption minimum, and V-TS2 and W-TS label the transition-state candidates for the two routes.

Acceptance required a converged optimisation, one chemically meaningful imaginary mode, and two displaced structures that relaxed towards different minima. The V-TS2 candidate for the first framework Al–O cleavage step met these Tier 1 criteria. Appendix A.3 records the candidate frequencies, displacement destinations, and final pathway classifications.

## 3.5 Gibbs Energies and Conditional Site Occupation

Harmonic Gibbs energies were obtained from ORCA frequency calculations at 298.15 K and 1003.15 K. The higher temperature represents an RFCC regenerator condition of about 730 °C. The Gibbs energy of adsorption was calculated using Eq. (3.3):

ΔG^{°}_{ads} = G^{°}(complex) − G^{°}(cluster) − G^{°}(adsorbate)
(3.3)

Equation (3.3) evaluates the complex against the separated cluster and isolated molecule at the same temperature. The calculations used ideal-gas, rigid-rotor, harmonic-oscillator thermochemistry at a standard pressure of 1 atm. Appendix B.4 shows the temperature-dependent substitutions for the two pre-reaction complexes.

A conditional competitive-site estimate was calculated at 1003.15 K using the RFCC gas partial pressures reported by Wormsbecher et al. (1986). At 2 atm total pressure, 20% steam gave a water partial pressure of 0.4 atm. At the same pressure, 1–10 ppm vanadic acid corresponded to partial pressures of 2 × 10⁻⁶ to 2 × 10⁻⁵ atm. Appendix B.1 shows these conversions. The dimensionless standard equilibrium constant for each adsorbate was calculated using Eq. (3.4). Equation (3.5) gives the fraction of sites occupied by each adsorbate:

K_{i}^{°} = exp[−ΔG^{°}_{i,ads}/(RT)]
(3.4)

θ_{i} = K_{i}^{°}(p_{i}/p^{°}) / [1 + Σ_{j} K_{j}^{°}(p_{j}/p^{°})]
(3.5)

The leading 1 in Eq. (3.5) represents an unoccupied site. The estimate used one equivalent site, ideal-gas standard states, and independent adsorbates. Equations (3.4) and (3.5) define a conditional model calculation for the stated gas composition. Appendix B.5 provides each numerical substitution and the resulting site fractions.

## 3.6 Data Handling and Acceptance Criteria

ORCA inputs, outputs, final geometries, and calculation registers were retained under `simulation/`. A minimum was classified from geometry convergence and its vibrational frequencies. Frequencies below 20 cm⁻¹ were treated as numerical noise when displacement analysis indicated flat cluster motion; borderline modes were assessed from their displacement behaviour. Transition-state candidates were assessed by optimisation convergence, mode count, and two-sided displacement. Appendix A provides the calculation register for the selected structural and pathway jobs.
