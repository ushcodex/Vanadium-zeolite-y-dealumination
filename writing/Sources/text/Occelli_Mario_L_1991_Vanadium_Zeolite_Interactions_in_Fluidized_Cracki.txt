I. INTRODUCTION 
Metal levels on equilibrium fluidized cracking catalysts (FCC) have been 
increasing through the years (Fig. l), indicating that the study of the physi- 
cochemical properties of metal resistant FCC has remained an important topic 
to refiners [l]. After the crude oil price collapse of 1986, Ni levels on equi- 
librium FCC have remained fairly constant whereas V levels have somewhat 
decreased owing to the greater availability (and lower cost) of high-quality 
crudes; Fig. 2. At the metals levels shown in Fig. 1, Ni and V catalyze the 
undesirable cracking of hydrocarbons in the gasoline boiling range, thus 
increasing coke and hydrogen yields. Vanadium-induced activity losses can 
be easily circumvented with the proper catalyst management and increased 
makeup rates. However, should refiners in the future undertake the cracking 
of residual oils or crack certain readily available crudes from Venezuela and 
Mexico, metals (especially V) will probably increase well above the levels 
shown in Fig. 1 and novel FCC will have to be found to negate the severe 
effects of V on cracking activity. 
It is well known that Ni (like Fe) does not quench cracking activity even 
when present at the 2-3% level [2-91. In contrast, above 0.5% V loading, 
catalyst properties deteriorate rapidly and at 1% V a near total loss of surface 
area and (useful) cracking activity is expected [7, lo]. At microactivity test 
conditions, it has been shown that addition of diluents capable of selectively 
sorbing metal contaminants from gas oils can form dual function cracking 
catalysts (DFCC) mixtures that retain most of their useful cracking activity 
even in the presence of 1.0-1.5% V [lo-131. The deleterious effects of V 
on cracking activity can be inhibited also by incorporating metal traps, such 
as sepiolite [14], titanates [15, 161, and sacrificial zeolites [17, 181 into the 
FCC matrix.

with metal levels above values currently found on equilibrium FCC. The

EXPERIMENTAL 
A . Catalyst Preparation and Testing 
The sample of calcined rare earth (RE) exchanged zeolite Y (CREY) and 
the high-activity cracking catalyst (GRZ-1) used in the present study were 
obtained from the Davison Company. Davison's GRZ-1 is an FCC that con- 
tains an estimated 35% CREY and after steam-aging has a BET surface area 
of 161 m2/g. The CREY sample had a Si02/A1203 ratio of 5.0; contained 
7.6% Ce2O3, 4.0% La20, 2.8% Nd203, and 0.9% Pr203; and had a BET 
surface area of 749 m2/g. A residual 3.5% Na20 is present in these crystals. 
The low (0.16%) Na20 REHY sample had BET surface area of 560 m2/g 
and SiO2/AI2O3 = 4.7. The crystals contained 0.91% Ce203, 5.2% La203, 
2.0% Nd203 and 0.71% Pr203. The HY sample (Linde LZY-82) had a 
SiO2/AI2O3 ratio of 5.4, its BET surface area was 761 m2/g, and it contained 
0.14% Na20. LaY (containing 8.1% La203) was obtained by reacting this 
HY with 1 M LaC13 solutions (prepared using Lac& * 7H20 crystals, from 
GFS Chemicals). The silicon-enriched HY crystals used (Linde LZ-210) had

B. Vanadium Analysis 
Vanadium in several aqueous extracts was determined by flame atomic 
emission spectrometry. The extracts were diluted with distilled water and 
potassium chloride was added as an ionization buffer. Atomic emission at 
437.92 nm was measured after aspirating the solutions into a fuel-rich nitrous 
oxide-acetylene flame. Quantitation was accomplished by comparing the sam- 
ple emission intensities with a calibration curve which was prepared from 
aqueous standard vanadium solutions. A polynomial regression was used to 
fit the vanadium standards to the calibration curve. 
C. Powder Diflraction Measurements 
Powder diffraction measurements were obtained with a Siemens D-500 
diffractometer at a scan rate of O.Ol"/step using 3 sec/step monochromatic 
CuK, radiation; CaF2 was used as an internal standard for angle calibration. 
Peak positions of the mullite samples were determined using computer pro- 
grams supplied by the instrument vendor and corrected to JCPDS CaF, p- 
sitions. Lattice parameters and cell volume were determined using a PC 
version of the Appleman refinement program as supplied by Benoit. Only 10 
to 15 reflections were used in the refinement due to the somewhat poor 
crystallinity and weak reflections present in some samples.

are stable at 540°C (Fig. 5-trace

perature is raised to 760"C, the faujasite structure collapses, and some mullite 
is formed, Fig. 5-B. 
Thus, even in the absence of steam, V-loaded Y zeolites 
collapse with an ease dependent largely on calcination temperatures and V

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS 
In a FCC unit, the catalyst from the regenerator, containing mainly V5+ 
compounds (such as supported V205), is admixed with fresh makeup catalyst 
Downloaded by [Michigan State University] at 12:12 13 June 2013 

<<<PAGE 37>>>
276 
OCCELLI 
and then recycled to the reactor zone (the riser) where gas oil cracking occurs 
and more V (probably as V02 +) and coke are deposited on the catalyst surface. 
From the riser, the spent catalyst goes to the steam-stripping zone where 
occluded hydrocarbons are removed from the catalyst surface. In the presence 
of steam, at 500-550°C, V impurities form volatile V compounds and other 
hydrolysis products which can react with the components (the matrix, clay, 
and zeolite) of an FCC, thus reducing catalyst activity and product selectiv- 
ities. 
In the hope of obtaining some insight into the possible nature of the 
reactions that may occur when V contaminated FCC are exposed to steam, 
the hydrolysis products of the reaction between water (at 25OC) and V con- 
taminated (and calcined) zeolites [such as HY (Linde’s LZY-82), silicon- 
enriched HY (Linde LZ-210), REHY, and CREY] have been examined by 
51V NMR. Results have indicated that formation of anions such as H2V04 
and HVOi-; condensation reaction products such as H2V20:-, HV20:-, 
and H2V20:-; and polyanions such as H2VI00& depend on contact times, 
catalyst composition, V concentration, and the pH of the liquid examined. 
During the time (30 to 60 sec) that spent catalysts spend in the steam- 
stripping zone, different reaction mechanisms and compounds are expected 
from the reaction between supported vanadium species and steam. However,
