# ABSTRACT

Vanadium contamination reduced the activity of Zeolite Y catalysts used in residue fluid catalytic cracking (RFCC). Proposed mechanisms assigned framework damage to vanadic acid formed from vanadium oxide and steam or to steam hydrolysis. This study compared vanadic acid (H₃VO₄) and water at a model Brønsted acid site using a 22-atom AlSi₄O₄H₁₃ cluster. Initial geometries were screened with GFN2-xTB, optimised with B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP, and checked with PBE0 single-point calculations. Harmonic Gibbs energies were calculated at 298.15 and 1003.15 K. B3LYP and PBE0 favoured vanadic acid electronically by 14.69 and 14.44 kJ mol⁻¹, respectively, while harmonic Gibbs energies favoured water by 11.82 and 66.36 kJ mol⁻¹ at the two temperatures. A formal competitive-site estimate using regenerator partial pressures gave a water-to-vanadic-acid occupation ratio of 5.7 × 10⁷ to 5.7 × 10⁸. The GFN2-xTB search yielded a 160.70 kJ mol⁻¹ candidate barrier for first Al–O cleavage; Appendix A records the DFT and water-path assessments. The results established a modest electronic binding preference and a conditional high-temperature site comparison. Larger pore-aware models and validated DFT reaction paths were recommended.
