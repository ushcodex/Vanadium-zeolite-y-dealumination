# ABSTRACT

Vanadium contamination reduced the activity of Zeolite Y catalysts used in residue fluid catalytic cracking. This study compared vanadic acid (H₃VO₄) and water adsorption at a model Brønsted acid site. A local cluster containing one framework aluminium atom and four silicon atoms was screened with GFN2-xTB, refined with B3LYP-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP, and checked using PBE0-D3(BJ)/def2-TZVP single-point calculations. Harmonic frequencies were used to assess the structures and calculate thermochemical corrections. B3LYP gave electronic adsorption energies of −93.0 kJ mol⁻¹ for vanadic acid and −78.3 kJ mol⁻¹ for water; PBE0 gave −93.1 and −78.6 kJ mol⁻¹, respectively. The electronic energies favoured vanadic acid by 14.7 and 14.4 kJ mol⁻¹, respectively. The harmonic free energies did not support a reliable estimate of site occupancy at regenerator temperature. The candidate density-functional structure for framework Al–O bond cleavage had fourteen imaginary frequencies, so no activation barrier was established. The calculations showed a modest electronic binding preference but did not establish that vanadic acid caused dealumination. Further work with a larger model and a validated reaction pathway was required.
